Plenty of cities book a big name for New Year's Eve. Austin is booking one of its own.

On Thursday, December 31, Shakey Graves headlines the 20th annual Austin's New Year at Auditorium Shores. The show is free, open to all ages, on the shore of the lake. At midnight, fireworks go up over Lady Bird Lake. CNN will broadcast the night live nationally, so the rest of the country gets a look at the skyline and the crowd that came to see it.

::pair

## The kid from the theater department

Alejandro Rose-Garcia, who performs as Shakey Graves, was born in Austin to parents who met in the University of Texas theater department. The stage name came from his time as a festival-goer at Old Settlers Music Festival, a longtime Austin event. He was an actor before he was a bandleader.

What he built from it is hard to mistake for anyone else. He made his name as a one-man band, playing guitar while stomping a kick drum made from an old suitcase. The songs pull from blues, folk, country and rock and roll. "Roll the Bones" and "Dearly Departed," his duet with Esmé Patterson, are two of his best-known songs.

The city noticed early. Austin proclaimed Shakey Graves Day on February 9, 2012, and he has been the pride of the city ever since.

## A quieter, newer record

His fifth album, Fondness, Etc., came out May 14, 2026. It is nine tracks about growing up and new parenthood, recorded on tape in his home studio. For a performer whose early reputation rested on a suitcase and a stomp, it is a notably domestic record, made at home about home.

He has been on tour behind it, and he recently played in Austin to officially open the Still Austin Quarter, alongside a DJ set by Adrian Quesada. On New Year's Eve he plays for the whole city at once.

## Local all the way down

The booking fits a pattern. The city has leaned toward artists with a certain nostalgia in recent years: Third Eye Blind led Austin into 2025, and The Head and the Heart took it into 2026. This year's choice is different in one way that counts. Every act on this year's stage calls Austin home, and additional local performers are still to be announced.

Mayor Kirk Watson put it this way in the announcement: "We're world-renowned for our food, culture, and music — and this New Year's party is another great opportunity to have a blast and to show off our beautiful city."

## What the night looks like

Gates open at 6 p.m. and the event runs until 12:30 a.m. Over ten local food trucks will be on site, and there are family-friendly activities for the younger crowd. The event is rain or shine, so dress for the weather.

This is also the first year VIP tickets are on sale. They cost $195 and come with a covered viewing deck with elevated sight lines to the main stage, free drinks (cocktails, beer, wine, non-alcoholic options and water), local food served late, heated restrooms and seating, and a dedicated VIP lane at both entrances. Everyone else gets in free.

::pair

## Getting there

Auditorium Shores is at 900 W. Riverside Dr., and the organizers are blunt about one thing: there is no parking at the event. A few ways to deal with that:

- Ride CapMetro: All CapMetro services, including MetroBus, MetroRapid and MetroRail, are free on New Year's Eve starting at 5 p.m.
- Walk or bike: Approach on foot or by bike, and bring a light for the Hike-and-Bike Trail after dark. Bike parking is available outside the gates.
- Share a ride: Rideshare pick-up and drop-off is at 100 Lee Barton Dr.
- Park and walk: Nearby garages include Palmer Events Center, One Texas Center, RiverSouth at Riverside and South First, and City Hall.

## What to bring, and what to leave

Every entrance has metal detectors and bag checks, and every bag is searched. Small clutch purses and fanny packs no bigger than 6 by 9 inches can use the express lanes, so travel light.

Allowed: strollers, blankets (no tarps or plastic sheeting), folding single-seat chairs, lunch-size coolers, handheld umbrellas and personal cameras, including DSLRs with standard lenses. Outside drinks are permitted only for childcare or medical needs.

Not allowed: outside alcohol, glass, drones, confetti and glitter, fireworks and sparklers, tents, pets (service animals excepted), skateboards and scooters, large bags, and smoking or vaping. Re-entry is allowed, through the main gates.

::band

## Why this one

A New Year's show on the lake is, by design, a communal thing. It works best when the person on stage belongs to the people in front of him. Shakey Graves grew up here, made his name here, and has toured for years. On December 31, he comes back to Auditorium Shores.

Austin's New Year runs Thursday, December 31, from 6 p.m. to 12:30 a.m. at Auditorium Shores. Details, the lineup and VIP tickets are at austinnewyear.com.
