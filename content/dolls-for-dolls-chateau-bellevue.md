Walk up to any holiday giving tree in December and notice which paper tags get picked first. Plush bears, board games and plastic trucks vanish in a week. The tags still hanging on the branches by mid-month almost always belong to older kids: a 12-year-old, a 15-year-old, a teenager who is too old for a plastic fire engine and too young to stop hoping something good is waiting on Christmas morning.

On Saturday, December 12, an annual downtown gathering puts those kids at the center of the room. From 3 to 6 p.m., the 10th Annual Dolls for Dolls Charity Mingle and Toy Drive takes over Chateau Bellevue, at 708 Nueces St. Admission is free. The ask at the door is simple: bring a new, unwrapped toy meant for a child or teen between the ages of 7 and 17.

## What happens inside

Most charity fundraisers ask you to buy a $150 gala ticket, sit through a chicken dinner and check your watch. Dolls for Dolls works more like an open house.

Families can stop by for portraits with Santa and Mrs. Claus, spend time at the Christmas craft and ornament-decorating tables, or step into a photo booth. Hot chocolate and holiday treats make the rounds while local business owners and neighbors mingle through the first-floor rooms. For anyone who wants to chip in a little more, there are raffle prizes and a silent auction; bringing an unwrapped gift for a child ages 7 to 17 also enters you into the drawing.

Because the drive targets 7-to-17-year-olds, it changes what ends up under the tree. Instead of infant rattles and toddler puzzles, the collection leans toward older youth — the group social workers and school counselors consistently say is hardest to shop for once the holidays arrive.

## Seventy-five years behind the drive

The afternoon benefits and is organized by the Austin Child Guidance Center, which is marking its 75th anniversary in 2026.

The center opened its doors in 1951, inspired by the national mental health reform movement that followed the Mental Health Reform Act of 1946. Community leaders saw a gap in Travis County and built Austin's first outpatient clinic dedicated solely to children's emotional well-being. From the beginning, it worked on a team model — pairing a psychiatrist, a psychiatric social worker and a psychologist around each child and family rather than treating a kid in isolation.

Three-quarters of a century later, from its permanent campus at 810 W. 45th St., the nonprofit provides accessible, trauma-informed therapy, psychological and neuropsychological testing, Spanish-language care and family support across Austin and Travis County. An afternoon of hot chocolate and ornament glue is a long way from an assessment room, which is half the point. Events like this keep the organization's work visible to neighbors who might never walk into a clinic — or might one day need to know it is there.

## A nineteenth-century castle on West Eighth

Even if you have walked past the corner of West Eighth and Nueces a hundred times, you may never have stepped past the iron fence.

Chateau Bellevue started in 1874 as Bellevue Place, built by Harvey and Catherine North on a limestone bluff overlooking Little Shoal Creek. Locals quickly nicknamed it Old North Castle. In 1892, Major Ira Hobart Evans bought the house and hired San Antonio architect Alfred Giles to remake it inside and out. Giles wrapped the stone house in French Romanesque arches, hand-carved cherry and walnut woodwork, stained glass and marble fireplaces painted with nursery rhymes and scenes from history.

In May 1929, a coalition of Austin women's clubs voted to buy the mansion from the Evans family for $36,000, creating the Austin Woman's Club. Nearly a century later, the nonprofit still owns and maintains the building, part of the National Register-listed Bremond Block Historic District, and offers discounted event rentals to local charitable groups so community fundraisers can afford a landmark room downtown.

For three hours on a Saturday afternoon, that means kids decorating ornaments under 19th-century arches while a high-rise skyline rises right behind the roof.

## If you go

The 10th Annual Dolls for Dolls Charity Mingle and Toy Drive runs Saturday, December 12, 2026, from 3 to 6 p.m. at Chateau Bellevue, 708 Nueces St. (at the corner of West Eighth Street) in downtown Austin. Admission is free; guests are asked to bring a new, unwrapped toy for a child or teen ages 7 to 17. Free registration is on Eventbrite, and more about the Austin Child Guidance Center's work is at austinchildguidance.org.
