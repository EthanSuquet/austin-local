On Thursday, November 19, the Austin City Council voted on a $25 million boardwalk without a word of debate. Both items rode the consent agenda, the part of a meeting reserved for things nobody expects to argue about.

That is a strange way to treat a project that took years of quiet wrangling to reach a vote.

## What was approved

The Riverside Shores Boardwalk would run about 760 feet along the south shore of Lady Bird Lake, between South Congress Avenue and South First Street. It passes the Hyatt Regency Austin and One Lady Bird Lake, an 18-story residential tower still under construction and due to finish in 2028.

The money comes from the Texas Department of Transportation. In 2023 the state promised $25 million as part of the package meant to soften the multibillion-dollar expansion of I-35 through Central Austin. The city separately took nearly $17.6 million earlier this year to compensate for parkland the highway affects. The boardwalk goes beyond what federal law requires.

Council's two votes accepted the state funding and put the Austin Public Facilities Corporation, a city-created entity formed in 2023, in charge of designing and building the project, with oversight from Austin Parks and Recreation. José Velásquez voted against both items. Marc Duchen abstained.

## The problem it is meant to fix

Anyone who has run, biked or pushed a stroller along this stretch of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail knows the spot. The city's own description calls it a pinch point: the path narrows, takes a sharp turn, climbs a steep grade, crosses boat dock operations, and suffers from pooling stormwater and eroding bank.

The plan widens the trail to 14 feet, with 2-foot shoulders on each side. It adds erosion stabilization and then the boardwalk itself, a second route over the water. For a trail that carries walkers, cyclists and runners at once, that is a real fix.

## Who pays, and who runs it

The agreement leaves the city responsible for any cost above $25 million, and unspent money returns to the state. City documents say the project will be designed to fit the budget and that no additional city funds are needed.

After construction, Austin Transportation and Public Works will maintain the boardwalk's infrastructure. The Trail Conservancy will handle operations, general maintenance and programming.

## How it got here

Nobody tells this part on a ribbon-cutting day. The Trail Conservancy, the nonprofit that helps maintain and improve the Butler Trail, worked with One Lady Bird Lake's developer, Related Companies, on an early concept. It became part of TxDOT's plans in 2023.

Then came a contest over control. TxDOT and the Conservancy spent months looking for a way to send the money straight to the nonprofit. The state agency's own contract lawyers said it lacked the authority to simply pick a nonprofit for the work. A second idea, using the Public Facilities Corporation as a middleman to pass the money along without a council vote, also collapsed.

Records obtained by KUT News show senior parks officials were frustrated at being left out of early talks. Some questioned whether a boardwalk was needed at all and tried to steer the money to parks more directly hit by the highway work. They did not succeed. In February, TxDOT wrote to Mayor Kirk Watson warning that if Austin refused the agreement, the project would come off its environmental commitments for the expansion. Take the deal or lose the money.

## The people who asked for a pause

Several speakers urged council to wait. Paul Saldaña, a founder of Hispanic Advocates Business Leaders of Austin, argued the money would do more good in East Austin parks, where residents live with the highway's direct effects. "Decades of segregation and under-investment have left these areas with fewer amenities, while rapid development now drives displacement," he told council.

The sharpest worry came from a business already on the water. Shannon Shaddock owns Capital Cruises, which has run boat tours and rentals on Lady Bird Lake since 1991. "While we support improving the south shore, the current plans for the boardwalk project seem to threaten to put us out of business," he said, asking the city to bring waterfront businesses into the design.

Mayor Pro Tem Chito Vela was the only council member to speak to the boardwalk before the vote. He asked city staff to protect access for the boat tours, even if that means finding another place for boats to launch.

## What happens next

The next step is public. The Austin Public Facilities Corporation board meets Monday, November 30, at 1 p.m. in Room 109 of Town Lake Center, 721 Barton Springs Road. Its agenda includes authorizing the corporation to deliver the boardwalk, at a cost not to exceed $25 million. Public comment is allowed in person or by phone, and phone speakers must register by noon the day before.

No construction date has been set. Parks spokesperson Dan Manco says the department is early in the process and expects a clearer picture of timelines within the next few months.

## The part worth watching

A boardwalk over Lady Bird Lake will be a good place to walk. It will also sit in front of a hotel and a luxury tower whose developer began selling condos last month, and it will displace or reshape a business that has been there for 35 years. Both can be true.

What the next few months settle is who gets a say in the design: the corporation that now holds the money, the Conservancy that will run the place, the boat operators on the water, or the residents who asked for a pause. The vote is done. The design is where this gets decided.
